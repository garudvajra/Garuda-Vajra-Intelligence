### 1. Executive Summary

This report reviews an official cybersecurity advisory published by the Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) on 10 May 2025.

The advisory identifies several categories of cyber threats relevant to industry, including ransomware attacks, distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) incidents, website defacements, data breaches and malware infections.

CERT-In recommends strengthening authentication and access control, enforcing multifactor authentication (MFA), applying security updates and implementing appropriate access restrictions.

This report summarises the advisory and interprets its defensive implications. It does not claim to measure the overall prevalence of cyberattacks in India or attribute the activity to a specific threat actor.

### 2. Research Questions

- Which categories of cybersecurity threats are identified in the selected official advisory?
- What defensive measures does CERT-In recommend to organisations?
- What can this single source establish, and what remains unverified?

### 3. Scope and Methodology

This is a preliminary, single-source open-source intelligence (OSINT) review.

The primary source is a publicly accessible CERT-In advisory. The document was reviewed for its stated threat categories, intended audience and recommended security controls.

The analysis distinguishes between:

- **Reported information:** Statements explicitly made in the advisory.
- **Analytical assessment:** Defensive implications inferred from the reported information.
- **Unknowns:** Questions that cannot be answered using this source alone.

No independent incident dataset, victim list, malware sample or threat-actor attribution has been established for this report.

### 4. Findings

#### Finding 1: Multiple categories of cyber threats are identified

**Evidence:** CERT-In's advisory, dated 10 May 2025, identifies ransomware attacks, DDoS incidents, website defacements, data breaches and malware infections as cybersecurity threats affecting business operations.

**Assessment:** Organisations should consider the confidentiality, integrity and availability of their systems when planning security controls and incident response.

**Limitations:** The selected advisory does not, by itself, establish the number of incidents, the rate of increase across India, the sectors most affected or the identity of responsible threat actors.

#### Finding 2: Authentication and access control are important defensive priorities

**Evidence:** CERT-In recommends strong, unique passwords, multifactor authentication and role-based access control.

**Assessment:** These controls can reduce exposure to compromised credentials and limit unnecessary access to systems and information.

**Limitations:** This report has not assessed the implementation of these controls within any specific organisation.

#### Finding 3: Security updates and patch management are recommended

**Evidence:** CERT-In advises organisations to regularly update operating systems, applications and security tools.

**Assessment:** Maintaining supported, updated software is an important part of reducing exposure to known security weaknesses.

**Limitations:** This review does not identify or test vulnerabilities in any particular organisation's infrastructure.

### 5. Defensive Recommendations

Based on the selected advisory, organisations should consider the following actions:

1. Enable multifactor authentication, particularly for privileged and remotely accessible accounts.
2. Use strong, unique passwords and appropriate access permissions.
3. Apply security updates to operating systems, applications and security tools in a timely manner.
4. Maintain tested backups and documented recovery procedures.
5. Monitor official cybersecurity advisories and review relevant recommendations.
6. Maintain an incident response process that defines responsibilities, escalation procedures and evidence preservation.

These are general defensive recommendations. Organisations should adapt them to their systems, operational requirements and risk assessments.

### 6. Confidence Assessment

**Confidence in the summary of the selected advisory: High.**

**Confidence in broader conclusions about the Indian cyber threat landscape: Low / Insufficient evidence.**

**Rationale:** The primary source is an official CERT-In publication. However, this report uses only one advisory and does not independently verify incident counts, trends, affected sectors or threat-actor attribution.

Confidence applies to the summary of what the source states, not to claims that the report has independently established the scale of cyber threats in India.

### 8. Limitations

This report is based on one publicly available advisory. It is not a comprehensive survey of cyber incidents in India and does not establish statistical trends, specific victim identities or attribution to a threat actor.

The findings should be interpreted within the limits of the cited source. Additional advisories, independently documented incidents and reputable technical research would be required to develop a broader assessment.
